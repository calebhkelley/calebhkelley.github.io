I teach college students. I teach them Spanish, sometimes linguistics,
and increasingly, life skills like how to send an email with a subject
line. My students are mostly Gen Z, which means they were born somewhere
between the invention of the iPhone and the collapse of attention spans
as we once knew them. They are kind, socially conscious, and
devastatingly proficient at Canva. They also, somehow, cannot read a
syllabus to save their lives.

Here's the thing: I love Gen Z. I admire their queerness, their candor,
their trauma literacy. They are earnest, ironic, and allergic to
pretense. But they also treat due dates like loose suggestions. They
submit essays at 11:59 PM not because they're procrastinators, but
because they've been raised by an algorithm that trained them to wait
for the deadline buzzer like it's the start of a flash sale.

They are also, somehow, always tired. It doesn't matter if it's 10 a.m.
or 3 p.m. or if we've just returned from spring break---someone will be
clutching an iced coffee like it's a flotation device and saying, with
great solemnity, "It's been a week." (It's Tuesday.)

They ask if they can reschedule office hours, as if they're salon
appointments. They send DMs on Canvas at 2:14 a.m. asking if I "could
maybe take a quick look" at their paper that's due at 8. They call me
"bestie" in emails and sign off with things like "xoxo" or "slay," and I
wish I were exaggerating.

They are, frankly, adorable. They care deeply about mental health,
pronouns, and whether the group project dynamic is "toxic." They are
fluent in aesthetic and allergic to ambiguity. They want rubrics for
everything---including how to have a hard conversation. They'll submit
gorgeous, color-coded slides with impeccable fonts and cite three
podcasts and a TikTok before they actually get to their thesis.

And bless them, they genuinely want to get it right. They want feedback
(as long as it's in sandwich form). They crave structure (as long as
it's optional). They struggle with boundaries, burnout, and basic file
naming conventions. And while they're certainly not the first generation
to avoid reading the textbook, they may be the first to cite burnout
from their internship, their part-time job, *and* their Discord
moderation duties as reasons why.

They are constantly anxious, perpetually exhausted, and
paradoxically---utterly uninterested in what they themselves claim to
care about. They want social justice but don't do the readings. They
want flexibility but freeze when asked to choose. They want their trauma
acknowledged, but please, not in red ink.

I've had students cry in my office because their group project didn't
"vibe." I've had students turn in work that's clearly heartfelt but
barely proofread, asking, "Does this count as turning something in?" And
often, they're trying. Really trying.

Let's be clear: it's not entirely their fault. We handed them a world
that's burning---ecologically, economically, politically---and then
asked them to read *Don Quijote* and reflect on the role of the pícaro
in post-imperial Spanish identity. Their lives are curated but chaotic,
hyperconnected but profoundly isolated. They are brilliant but brittle.
And I worry.

I worry that in trying to protect them from everything---stress,
failure, discomfort---we've rendered them unable to cope with anything.
I worry that we professors (myself included) have traded rigor for
relevance, clarity for compassion, until we're just customer service
agents in cardigans. I worry that a generation raised on infinite
scrolling can no longer sit with a difficult idea for more than 30
seconds without looking for the comments section.

Still, there is hope. They are funny. They are weird. They care. And
every now and then, they say something so piercing and original that it
realigns my faith in the whole profession. When they do show up---in
body, mind, and spirit---it's electric.

So yes, Gen Z students are a bit chaotic. They will forget the readings,
oversleep their presentations, and send emails that begin, "Heyyyy quick
q lol." But they are also kind, brave, and oddly hopeful. They show
up---with anxiety, with snacks, with memes---and they are trying. Really
trying.

And if I'm honest, I'm rooting for them.

But for the love of all that is holy---*read the damn syllabus.*
