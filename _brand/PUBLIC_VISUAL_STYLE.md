# Public visual style guide

The look for Caleb H. Kelley’s public-facing graphics, starting with the Substack (@calebhkelley, “The Sound & Social Life of Language”) and its Instagram and LinkedIn carousels. Set on 14 November 2026 from the first carousel, on syllable-final /s/ in Seville.

This file is the master copy. Other repos point here rather than keeping their own copy.

## Scope

Use it for social posts, Substack graphics, talk title slides meant for sharing, and other public science communication. Course materials keep the Estilo 2027 system in `proposed-courses/STYLE_GUIDE.md` and the SPAN repos. Journal figures follow each journal’s rules.

## The feel

Crisp, bright, and cool. Blues, greens, silvers, and grays on white. Nothing vintage, tan, or cream. Brown appears only as the rare, smallest accent, never as text and never as a fill.

## Color

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | `#FFFFFF` | Background, always white |
| Ink | `#0E1A2B` | Headlines, body text, the top band, the handle |
| Muted | `#4A5568` | Captions and secondary labels |
| Panel | `#EDF1F7` | Diagram cards and spectrogram panels |
| Example | `#00794F` | Emerald, marks every “For example” label and line, and the matching highlights in diagrams |
| Why it matters | `#2250D9` | Cobalt, marks every “Why it matters” label and line |

Accent colors always mean the same thing. Emerald is the example, cobalt is why it matters, and neither is used for anything else. Every text color passes WCAG 2.2 AA (4.5:1) on both white and the panel color. Labels are white text on a solid emerald or cobalt pill.

## Type

| Role | Typeface | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Headlines | DM Serif Display | Regular weight, about 88 px on a 1080 px slide, numbered (“1. Indexicality”) |
| Body, labels, diagrams | Atkinson Hyperlegible Next | Regular and bold, body about 32 px, example and why lines about 30 px |

Both are free on Google Fonts under the SIL Open Font License. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next comes from the Braille Institute and is drawn so that easily confused letters (I, l, 1 and 0, O) stay distinct, which is why its zero carries a slash.

### Type rules

- Sentence case everywhere. No small letterspaced all-caps labels.
- Italicize Spanish words in English text (*las casas*).
- Bold only for the series line, the handle, labels, and highlighted segments.
- Left-aligned text, never justified.

## Carousel layout

Portrait 4:5 at 1080 × 1350 px, which suits both Instagram and LinkedIn. Padding of 88 px top, 96 px sides, and 60 px bottom. Each slide is one numbered concept, top to bottom.

1. A 14 px ink band across the full top edge.
2. A series line in bold ink (“The Sound & Social Life of Language”) with the slide count at the right (“1 / 5”).
3. The numbered serif headline.
4. Two or three lines of plain body text.
5. The emerald “For example” pill, then one everyday example in emerald.
6. One diagram on panel-colored cards, with highlights in emerald, and a short muted caption.
7. The cobalt “Why it matters” pill, then one or two lines in cobalt.
8. A thin ink arrow rule, which becomes a plain rule on the last slide.
9. The handle, @calebhkelley, centered in bold ink.

## Diagrams and research ethics

- Spectrograms are drawn schematically and captioned “synthesized for illustration.” Friction noise for [s] and [h] is drawn in emerald.
- Never use audio, spectrograms, or quotes from IRB participants.
- Never present unpublished findings from the Seville F0 or vowel space manuscripts.
- Illustrative positions or scales get a caption that says so (“Illustrative positions, not measured data”).

## Writing on graphics

Caleb’s usual rules apply. No em dashes. No colons to bridge ideas or introduce topics. Curly apostrophes and quotation marks only. Oxford comma in English, never in Spanish. No “it’s not X, it’s Y” or “more than just” framing. No sweeping abstract closings. Never tell students to use vosotros.
